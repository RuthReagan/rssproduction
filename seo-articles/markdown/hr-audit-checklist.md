# The HR Audit: A Practical Checklist for Reducing Risk

_An HR audit finds compliance gaps and risks before they become lawsuits or penalties. Use this HR audit checklist to review policies, records, pay practices, and more._

An HR audit is a structured review of your people practices, policies, records, pay, hiring, and compliance, designed to find problems while they are still cheap to fix. Most organizations do not discover their HR gaps until a claim, an agency notice, or a bad hire forces the issue. An audit flips that: you find the exposure on your own terms and close it before it costs you.

## What an HR audit covers

A thorough audit looks across the entire employment lifecycle. On the compliance side that means required postings, personnel and I-9 records, wage-and-hour practices, worker classification, and leave administration. On the practices side it means hiring and onboarding, performance management, discipline, and terminations.

It also reviews the documents that tie it all together, your handbook, offer letters, job descriptions, and policies, to make sure what is written matches what you actually do.

## The compliance essentials to check

Start with the items that carry the steepest penalties: I-9 completion and storage, exempt versus non-exempt classification, overtime calculation, and final-pay handling. Confirm required federal and state postings are current and that new-hire reporting is happening.

Review leave compliance against FMLA and any state leave laws, ADA accommodation processes, and harassment-prevention training requirements. These are the areas where agencies and plaintiffs' attorneys look first.

## Records and documentation

Personnel files should be complete, current, and properly segregated, medical information and I-9s kept separate from the main file. Check that you are retaining records for the legally required periods and disposing of them appropriately after.

Documentation gaps are quietly dangerous. If you cannot show a performance history, a signed handbook acknowledgment, or the basis for a pay decision, you lose the ability to defend it later.

## Pay practices and classification

Pay is where audits most often uncover real money at risk. Verify that exempt employees genuinely meet the duties and salary tests, that non-exempt employees are paid correctly for all hours including overtime, and that any independent contractors are properly classified.

Also review pay equity and transparency. A growing number of states require salary ranges in postings and restrict certain pay practices, and these rules carry penalties and reputational risk when ignored.

## Turning findings into action

An audit is only useful if it drives change. The right output is a prioritized list, what is urgent and high-risk, what is important but not immediate, and what is a nice-to-have, so leadership can fix the dangerous items first.

Many organizations run an audit with outside HR support precisely so the findings come with a clear remediation plan and, where needed, help executing it, rather than a report that simply lists problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should we conduct an HR audit?

A full audit every one to two years is a good baseline, with a lighter annual check on high-risk areas like classification, I-9s, and required postings. Organizations that are growing fast, entering new states, or facing a lot of change benefit from auditing more often.

### Should an HR audit be done internally or by an outside expert?

Internal reviews are valuable, but an outside expert brings objectivity and sees issues an internal team may be too close to notice. Many organizations use both: internal checks throughout the year and a periodic independent audit for a fresh, defensible assessment.

### What is the single most common issue HR audits find?

Worker misclassification and wage-and-hour errors are among the most common and most expensive findings, followed closely by incomplete I-9s and outdated policies. These are also some of the most straightforward to fix once identified.

### Will an audit disrupt our operations?

A well-run audit is designed to minimize disruption. Most of the work involves reviewing documents and practices, with limited time required from your team. The short investment is far less costly than the disruption of a claim or agency investigation.

## Key Takeaway

An HR audit lets you find and fix compliance gaps on your own terms, before they turn into penalties or lawsuits. Review compliance, records, and pay practices regularly, prioritize the highest-risk findings, and treat the audit as the start of action, not just a report.
